# Rectified flow distillation of the FLUX SFT model: autoresearch benchmark

## Summary

The SFT EMA model (20 Euler steps, sigma shift 2.0) was distilled with rectified flow into a
student that samples in 1 to 4 uniform Euler steps. The student keeps almost all of the teacher's
quality at 5x to 20x fewer network evaluations.

- Native 256 px patch FID, the primary metric: teacher **0.8241**; student **0.8413** (1 step),
  **0.8378** (2), **0.8386** (3), **0.8407** (4). The gap is +1.7% to +2.1%. The student recovers
  98.4% to 98.7% of the patch FID improvement that SFT gave over the stage-2 pretrained model
  (1.9031 -> 0.8241).
- Whole-image FID is the weakest metric at 1 step (**+9.6%**). At 2 to 4 steps it is within
  **+2.7% to +3.3%** of the teacher.
- Image-space MSE is **+1.0% to +1.4%** (high resolution) and **+2.0% to +3.6%** (low resolution)
  above the teacher. High-resolution latent MSE is within **-0.2% to +0.9%**.
- There is no clear gain from 3 or 4 steps over 2. 2 steps is the best on patch FID and
  high-resolution image MSE; 3 steps is the best on whole-image FID.
- Per-step cost, memory and architecture are unchanged, so sampling cost scales with the step
  count: **19.7x (1 step), 9.8x (2), 6.6x (3), 4.9x (4)** faster than the 20-step teacher.

These are in-domain results: the benchmark references are generated by the FLUX teacher, and the
distillation pairs come from the SFT model that was trained on FLUX samples. They do not by
themselves establish the same behavior on real-image datasets.

## Setup

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Teacher | SFT EMA, `sft_runs/flux2_v2/export/sft_ema.safetensors`; 20 Euler steps, sigma shift 2.0 |
| Student | `distill_runs/distill_sft_v1/export/student_ema.safetensors` (EMA weights) |
| Student initialization | Teacher weights |
| Distillation data | 13,200 teacher pairs (noise -> teacher latents), one pair per SFT block over all 8 resolution buckets; text and high-frequency conditioning dropped with the SFT probabilities |
| Distillation training | One epoch, 1,650 steps of 8 samples, LR 1e-4 with linear decay over the last 30% |
| Training noise levels | 1 to 4 step uniform schedules; pure noise (t = 1) gets 50% of the samples, the rest is split equally between the 2, 3 and 4 step schedules |
| Student sampling | 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 uniform Euler steps (sigma shift 1.0), seed 0, bf16 |
| Benchmark | Unmodified `scripts/run_benchmark.py` on `benchmark_datasets/flux_1024`, all 1,000 samples, same settings as `reports/sft_vs_stage2_pretrain.md` |
| Conditioning | Benchmark SDXL prompt embeddings; high-frequency conditioning disabled (null embeddings) |
| Hardware | One NVIDIA RTX 5090 |

### Reading the results

- Patch FID, which measures detail statistics, changes least. A distilled student reproduces the
  teacher's fine-detail distribution at every step count.
- The 1-step student has a larger whole-image FID gap (+9.6%) than the 2 to 4 step students
  (+2.7% to +3.3%). One step costs global-statistics quality that a second step recovers, while the
  detail metrics are nearly unaffected.
- All students stay slightly behind the teacher on image-space MSE. The 1-step student has the
  lowest high-resolution latent MSE of all, including the teacher, but its decoded-image metrics
  are not better, as already seen in the SFT comparison, where latent MSE moved against the image
  metrics.
- Against the raw (non-EMA) SFT model (patch FID 0.8990, FID 0.9790, HR image MSE 0.00187713), every
  student is better on patch FID and high-resolution image MSE. The 2 to 4 step students match its
  whole-image FID, and the 1 step student is worse (1.0402).

## Efficiency results

The student has the same architecture as the teacher, so one forward pass costs the same and
sampling cost is the step count times that. Latencies are from the benchmark's compiled
single-forward measurement (ms, average); sampling latency is the derived total for the
UNet only, without VAE or text encoding.

| Model | Steps | Forward 512 | Forward 1024 | Forward 2048 | Sampling 1024 | Speedup vs teacher |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SFT EMA (teacher) | 20 | 11.808 | 41.901 | 216.137 | 838.0 | 1.0x |
| Distilled student | 1 | 12.242 | 42.648 | 219.444 | 42.6 | 19.7x |
| Distilled student | 2 | 12.290 | 42.900 | 219.900 | 85.8 | 9.8x |
| Distilled student | 3 | 12.124 | 42.557 | 217.883 | 127.7 | 6.6x |
| Distilled student | 4 | 12.154 | 42.566 | 217.874 | 170.3 | 4.9x |

Peak memory is identical to the teacher at 512 / 1024 / 2048 px (997 / 1408 / 3049 MB) within 4 MB.
Forward-latency differences of up to 4.1% (at 512 px) come from measurement variation, not from the
model; speedups use each model's own measured forward time. At 2048 px the speedups are the same
within 0.1x.

## Scope and interpretation

- Single-seed, one-time evaluation with no repeated runs or confidence intervals. The differences
  between the 2, 3 and 4 step students (about 0.3% in patch FID, 0.5% in whole-image FID) should not
  be used to rank them; only the 1-step whole-image FID gap is clearly larger.
- The EMA student was evaluated; the raw student weights were not benchmarked.
- The student can at best reproduce its teacher, so the comparison measures how much the 20-step
  SFT model loses by being compressed into a few steps, not whether it beats a real-image baseline.
  `axis_1024` or another real-image benchmark is needed for out-of-domain behavior.
- The student must be sampled with uniform sigmas (shift 1.0), the noise levels it was trained on;
  the 20-step teacher uses shift 2.0.
