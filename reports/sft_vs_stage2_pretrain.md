# FLUX SFT versus stage-2 pretraining: autoresearch benchmark

## Summary

The 20,000-step FLUX SFT model substantially improves the autoresearch `flux_1024` quality metrics
over the final stage-2 pretrained model, with no architectural or inference-cost change.

- Primary metric, native 256 px patch FID:
  - raw: **1.8910 -> 0.8990**, a **52.5% reduction**;
  - EMA: **1.9031 -> 0.8241**, a **56.7% reduction**.
- Whole-image FID falls by **31.6%** for raw and **34.7%** for EMA.
- High-resolution and low-resolution image MSE also improve. High-resolution latent MSE is the
  exception: it increases by **3.5%** for raw and **3.7%** for EMA.
- **SFT EMA is the best of the four evaluated models.** EMA is slightly worse than raw on patch
  FID before SFT (+0.6%), but better after SFT (-8.3%).
- Single-forward latency and peak memory are unchanged within measurement variation.

These are in-domain results: both the SFT data and benchmark references are generated by the FLUX
teacher. They do not by themselves establish the same gain on real-image datasets.

## Evaluation setup

The evaluation used the unmodified `scripts/run_benchmark.py` and its standard non-smoke settings:

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Dataset | `benchmark_datasets/flux_1024`, all 1,000 samples |
| References | FLUX.2-klein-4B teacher-generated 1024 x 1024 images |
| Primary quality metric | FID on non-overlapping native-resolution 256 x 256 tiles |
| Sampling | 20 Euler steps, sigma shift 2.0, seed 0, bf16 |
| Conditioning | Benchmark SDXL prompt embeddings; high-frequency conditioning disabled (null embeddings) |
| Efficiency | 50 compiled single-forward latency iterations at 512 / 1024 / 2048 output px |
| Memory | Full eager `upscale` profiling, including weights |
| Hardware | One NVIDIA RTX 5090 |

The four runs completed successfully in 74.8 minutes. No smoke evaluation was used.

Checkpoints:

- Stage 2, step 54,108:
  `pretrain_runs/stage2_v1/export/stage2_final_000054108_{raw,ema}.safetensors`.
- SFT, step 20,000: `sft_runs/flux2_v2/export/sft_{raw,ema}.safetensors`.
  Both exports were verified to match `checkpoints/checkpoint_000020000.pt` exactly, tensor for
  tensor.

## Quality results

All metrics are lower-is-better. `HR image MSE` compares decoded 1024 px outputs with the teacher
images. `LR image MSE` compares their 2x bilinear downscales with the benchmark inputs. FID and
MSE are computed over the same 1,000 samples for every model.

| Stage | Weights | Patch FID | FID | HR image MSE | LR image MSE | HR latent MSE |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stage-2 pretrain | Raw | 1.8910 | 1.4308 | 0.00219172 | 0.00070058 | 0.29509 |
| Stage-2 pretrain | EMA | 1.9031 | 1.4535 | 0.00216347 | 0.00069991 | 0.29354 |
| FLUX SFT 20k | Raw | **0.8990** | 0.9790 | 0.00187713 | 0.00062667 | 0.30532 |
| FLUX SFT 20k | EMA | **0.8241** | **0.9488** | **0.00184074** | **0.00061787** | 0.30440 |

### Matched change from pretraining to SFT

The delta is `SFT - pretrain`; negative values are improvements.

| Metric | Raw delta | Raw change | EMA delta | EMA change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Patch FID | -0.99201 | **-52.46%** | -1.07900 | **-56.70%** |
| FID | -0.45181 | **-31.58%** | -0.50470 | **-34.72%** |
| HR image MSE | -0.00031459 | **-14.35%** | -0.00032273 | **-14.92%** |
| LR image MSE | -0.00007391 | **-10.55%** | -0.00008204 | **-11.72%** |
| HR latent MSE | +0.01023 | **+3.47%** | +0.01085 | **+3.70%** |

### EMA effect within each stage

| Stage | Patch FID: EMA vs raw | FID: EMA vs raw | HR image MSE: EMA vs raw |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Stage-2 pretrain | +0.01215 (+0.64%) | +0.02265 (+1.58%) | -0.00002825 (-1.29%) |
| FLUX SFT 20k | -0.07485 (-8.33%) | -0.03023 (-3.09%) | -0.00003639 (-1.94%) |

The large patch-FID reduction shows better generated detail statistics, rather than only a global
color or layout improvement. The reductions in whole-image FID and both pixel-space MSE metrics
show that global structure and low-frequency fidelity improve at the same time. Latent MSE moves
in the opposite direction, so SFT does not improve pointwise latent reconstruction even though the
decoded-image metrics improve substantially.

## Efficiency results

Latency is the average single-forward time in milliseconds; peak memory is in MB and includes the
model weights. All variants have the same architecture and sampler, so equal costs are expected.

| Stage | Weights | Latency 512 | Latency 1024 | Latency 2048 | Peak 512 | Peak 1024 | Peak 2048 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stage-2 pretrain | Raw | 11.883 | 42.161 | 218.301 | 997.09 | 1407.53 | 3049.28 |
| Stage-2 pretrain | EMA | 11.820 | 42.013 | 216.302 | 997.09 | 1407.53 | 3049.28 |
| FLUX SFT 20k | Raw | 11.968 | 42.071 | 216.066 | 997.09 | 1407.53 | 3049.28 |
| FLUX SFT 20k | EMA | 11.808 | 41.901 | 216.137 | 997.09 | 1407.53 | 3049.28 |

Across all four runs, average-latency spread is at most 1.36% at any resolution, and peak memory is
identical. SFT therefore moves the quality frontier without a measured efficiency regression.

## Scope and interpretation

- The benchmark uses separate generation seeds (`1,000,000 + sample index`) from the SFT training
  blocks, but its references and the SFT samples share the same FLUX teacher distribution. The
  measured gain is strong evidence of in-domain improvement; `axis_1024` or another real-image
  benchmark is needed to measure generalization.
- These values must not be compared directly with the final 1.721 / 1.736 patch FID in
  `reports/stage2_pretrain_report.md`. That report used a different held-out ReLAION benchmark and
  enabled target high-frequency conditioning; this evaluation uses FLUX references and null
  high-frequency conditioning, matching the autoresearch interface and inference example.
- This was a one-time, single-seed evaluation with no repeated runs or confidence intervals. Small
  latency differences should be treated as noise. The main SFT quality differences are much larger
  than the run-to-run efficiency variation.

## Artifacts and reproduction

- One-time driver: `dev/run_sft_pretrain_benchmark.py`.
- SFT benchmark entries: `dev/pretrain_eval/sft_flux2_v2_20k_{raw,ema}.py`.
- Log: `dev/evaluations/sft_pretrain_benchmark/benchmark.log`.
- Full metrics, profiles, and 32 saved input/output pairs per model:
  `raw_results/{pretrain_stage2_final,sft_flux2_v2_20k}_{raw,ema}/`.

To rerun completed models, remove their corresponding result directories first; the driver skips
any model whose `metrics.json` already exists. Then run:

```bash
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 .venv/bin/python -u dev/run_sft_pretrain_benchmark.py
```
